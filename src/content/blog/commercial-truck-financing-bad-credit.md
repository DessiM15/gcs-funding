A bank looks at a credit report and makes a decision. An equipment lender looks at a credit report, the asset, the down payment, and what you actually do for a living, and then makes a decision.

That difference is why files that are dead at a bank are routinely fundable through second-chance equipment programs.

We have been financing hard assets for over twenty years. Here is how it works when the credit is not clean.

## What actually disqualifies a deal, and what does not

Things that show up constantly on files we fund:

- Chapter 7 or Chapter 13 bankruptcy
- Tax liens
- Judgments
- Repossessions
- Slow pays and collections
- Startups with no time in business

None of those is automatically fatal. They change the structure, not the outcome.

What genuinely makes a deal hard is a combination: no down payment, no industry experience, no verifiable income, and an asset the lender cannot easily remarket. Fix any one of those and the file gets meaningfully better.

## The three levers that get a challenged file approved

### 1. Down payment

This is the biggest one. Second-chance programs typically require more down than a clean-credit deal, and that is the trade: the lender's exposure drops, and your approval odds rise. If credit is the problem, cash is the answer.

### 2. The asset itself

Lenders think about what happens if they end up owning the truck. A common spec, in demand, with reasonable miles and a clear title is easier to approve than something niche. We finance new and used equipment from dealers, private sellers, manufacturers, and auction houses, so the source is flexible, but the asset still matters.

### 3. Experience

An owner-operator with eight years driving and a repossession is a fundamentally different risk from a first-time buyer with the same repossession. Experience in the industry you are financing into carries real weight, and it is worth stating clearly on the application rather than assuming it is obvious.

## How fast it moves

Approvals come back **within 3 to 4 hours of a complete application package**. That phrase does the work: complete. The single largest cause of delay is a package missing documents, which puts the file at the back of the queue every time it is touched.

Once the borrower accepts the approval, loan documents are expedited directly to the dealer or buyer.

## Getting the package right the first time

Before you apply, have ready:

- Basic details on you and the business
- Bank statements
- Details on the specific unit, including year, make, model, mileage, VIN, and the seller
- An honest account of the credit issues, including dates and whether they are discharged or satisfied

That last item is worth being direct about. Underwriters find everything. A discharged bankruptcy disclosed up front is a structuring conversation. The same bankruptcy discovered on the report after you did not mention it is a credibility problem, and credibility is most of what you are borrowing against when the score is low.

## The honest trade-off

Second-chance financing costs more than clean-credit financing. The rate is higher and the down payment is larger, because the risk is real and someone has to price it.

The question is not whether that is worse than a prime rate. It is whether the truck earns more than the payment. For most operators putting a productive asset to work, it does, and comfortably.
