Short answer: **yes, a Texas business can surcharge credit card transactions**, provided the program follows federal law and card brand rules. The longer answer matters more, because surcharging is one of those areas where a merchant can be technically well-intentioned and still end up out of compliance.

Texas once had a state statute restricting surcharges. It was ruled unenforceable against merchants in federal court, and the state has not enforced it since. What actually governs a surcharge program today is the card brand rules, plus federal law and the settlement agreements that created the practice in the first place.

Here is what that means in practice.

## The four rules that decide compliance

### 1. You must register with the card brands

A surcharge is not something you switch on at the terminal. The merchant has to be registered with the card networks before the first surcharged transaction runs. Skipping this is the most common way a program becomes non-compliant on day one, and it is the easiest to avoid.

### 2. You must disclose the fee clearly

Customers must be told about the fee before they pay. That means appropriate signage at the point of entry and the point of sale for a physical location, and clear disclosure on the checkout page for an online business. It also has to appear on the receipt.

Disclosure is not fine print. The rule exists so nobody is surprised at the register, and enforcement follows that intent.

### 3. The fee cannot exceed the cap, and cannot make you money

The surcharge is capped, and it must not exceed your actual cost of acceptance. The ceiling under card brand rules is 4%, and the fee may not profit the merchant. Under our program the cardholder pays a 3.5% program fee, which covers the processing cost, and the merchant receives 100% of the sale.

That last part is the point of the whole exercise. A surcharge is a cost recovery mechanism, not a revenue line.

### 4. Debit cards cannot be surcharged. Ever.

This is where good programs go wrong at scale. **Debit and prepaid card transactions may not carry a surcharge**, even when they are run as credit. If your terminal cannot reliably distinguish a debit card from a credit card at the moment of sale, you will eventually surcharge a debit transaction, and that is a violation.

This is not a discipline problem, it is an equipment problem. Surcharge-enabled terminals identify eligible transactions and apply the fee only where it belongs, which is why the hardware matters as much as the paperwork.

## What surcharging actually does to your numbers

A business running $45,000 a month in card volume at a 3.1% effective rate is spending roughly $1,395 a month on processing, or about $16,700 a year. If 70% of that volume is credit rather than debit, a compliant surcharge program moves the majority of that cost to the cardholders who chose to pay by credit.

You can model your own figures with our [surcharge savings calculator](/resources/surcharge-savings-calculator).

Two caveats worth stating honestly:

- **Your effective rate is probably higher than your quoted rate.** Divide total statement fees by total volume to get the real number.
- **Some customers will react.** Most do not, particularly when the disclosure is clean and a no-fee option like debit or cash is available. But a business with a high proportion of repeat, low-margin transactions should think about it rather than assume.

## Surcharge, cash discount, and dual pricing are not the same thing

These get used interchangeably, and they are not interchangeable.

| Model | How it works | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Surcharge** | A fee added to credit card transactions | Requires registration and disclosure. Debit exempt. Capped. |
| **Cash discount** | A lower price offered for cash | The posted price is the card price. Different rule set. |
| **Dual pricing** | Two prices displayed for every item | Must be shown consistently, everywhere. |

Programs that are marketed as one thing but implemented as another are where merchants get into trouble. If someone offers you a "0% processing" program without registering you with the card brands or explaining how debit is handled, ask more questions.
